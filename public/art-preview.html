Hammer

Icon concepts — something made by hand

Five attempts, each drawn from a different craft tradition. No gradients pretending to be depth, no generic node graphs. Real materials: iron and ash, sumi ink on paper, stained glass, kumiko joinery. Pick the one that feels like it could exist outside a screen.

Wrought Iron & Ash
A real claw hammer at an angle — chipped strike face, chamfered corner, wood grain in the handle, a couple of sparks off the striking edge. The dark ground is there so the metal reads as metal, not as a gradient box.
Material study · warm ground
Sumi on Washi
A single loaded brush stroke: heavy head, tapering handle, a drip at the end. Paper texture in the background, a small hanko seal in the corner. The mark is made, not assembled from shapes.
Brush & ink · pale ground
Cathedral Tracery
An arched iron window — mullions, a rose window with radiating lead lines, rust streaks on the frame, stone sill. The arch echoes the hammer head's curve; the structure is load-bearing, not decorative.
Ironwork & glass · dark ground
Rose Window
A six-petal tracery wheel in ruby, sapphire, amber, and emerald, leaded together around a brass hub. Takes the hammer's head-as-hub idea and pushes it into a craft that has been doing geometry since the 12th century.
Stained glass · dark ground
Kumiko Mallet
Iron frame with a wood panel laid in a kumiko grid — crossing diagonals, inlaid diamonds at each intersection, a brass pin at center. A mallet head sits through the middle. The joinery is the point, not the silhouette.
Wood & iron · kumiko
